# Five industry conversations — willingness-to-pay test

Status: **instrument ready; 0/5 completed**. A conversation counts only with a named role, date, consented notes and a direct response to the priced next step. Friendly interest is not willingness to pay.

## Hypothesis

A buyer with a costly, measurable operating problem will fund a small, falsifiable validation step when the brief fixes the criterion, makes sponsor inputs visible, and produces useful information even on failure.

## 30-minute script

1. “Tell me about the last time this problem happened.” Ask for frequency, impact, current workaround and decision owner.
2. “What have you already paid—money or team time—to address it?”
3. Show one problem brief without pitching the platform. Ask what is wrong or missing.
4. Show the best-matched asset and line-by-line gaps. Ask which gap kills the match.
5. Show the smallest validation step, including their inputs and the negative-result meaning.
6. Price test: “Would you approve ₹`X` for this step in the next 30 days?” If no: “At what amount, evidence level, or approval route would this become approvable?”
7. Ask for the real next action: introduce the budget owner, share a redacted sample, review an SOW, or decline.

Do not ask “Would you use this?” Do not count praise, a letter of intent without a decision route, or a hypothetical future budget as WTP.

## Classification

- `Strong`: agrees to a dated commercial next action controlled by the buyer—SOW review, procurement start, data-room access, or paid pilot.
- `Conditional`: names a concrete missing condition and the person/process that can clear it.
- `Weak`: general interest, no budget owner or dated action.
- `No`: problem, price, trust or route is not viable.

Exit criterion: at least 3/5 are Strong or Conditional, at least 2 name a feasible payment/procurement route, and the median approvable validation amount supports delivery cost. Otherwise revise the wedge before authoring more briefs.
